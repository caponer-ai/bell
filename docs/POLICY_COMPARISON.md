# What this project adds over five lines of local checking

Generated by `script/policy_comparison.py` from the 30 settlements in
[`replay_settlements.json`](replay_settlements.json). Staleness budget: **900 s** for every
policy, so the only differences below come from the rules themselves.

| Policy | Settles | Refuses |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1. As deployed (the market's own rule) | 30 | 0 |
| 2. Freshness check only | 3 | 27 |
| 3. Calendar + freshness + same-round refusal (a careful local patch) | 0 | 30 |
| 4. PushFeedGuard alone | 2 | 28 |
| 5. PushFeedGuard + SettlementPairGuard, as deployed here | 0 | 30 |

## The honest part

The per-read guard alone (policy 4) agrees with a careful local patch on **28 of
30** settlements; with the pair rule added (policy 5) they agree on **30 of 30**.

The two settlements where the local patch was **stricter than our guard**, which is how the pair
rule came to exist:
- v2 #25 PLTR, 4 s between the reads, price 8 min old,
  both reads inside the regular session: local says REFUSE (both reads from one round), the per-read guard says SETTLE.
- v2 #26 AMD, 11 s between the reads, price 4 min old,
  both reads inside the regular session: local says REFUSE (both reads from one round), the per-read guard says SETTLE.

A per-read check cannot see a relationship between two reads. `SettlementPairGuard` adds exactly
that rule and nothing else, and policy 5 is the result.


The calendar earns its place against the cheapest patch: **3 of 30**
settlements pass a pure freshness check and are still refused once a session calendar is applied.
Examples: PLTR regular, AMD regular, TSLA pre.

## What this does not prove

These 30 settlements are one market on one chain, most of them locked and settled within a minute
of each other, with 0.009 ETH ever staked across all of them. The table shows which rules would
have refused which settlement. It does not show that anyone lost money, that a refusal would have
produced a different winner, or that a consumer wants any of these rules.
